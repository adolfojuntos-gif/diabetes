Post 1 ~340 words

I built a diabetes app for people who are tired of logging numbers into nothing.

It's called Steady. Your diabetes, one day at a time.

Most apps are a diary. You type the number, the number goes in a list, and the list never tells you anything. Steady reads your own numbers back to you.

Here's the whole thing:

TODAY — your last reading, where today sits, the last 7 days, what needs attention, and the patterns your data actually supports. Not generic tips. Your Tuesdays.

LOG — glucose, meals, insulin, movement, sleep, water, symptoms, weight, blood pressure, medication taken. Snap a photo of your plate and it estimates the carbs. Already wear a CGM? Import the file from your meter or sensor instead of typing.

TRENDS — time in range, your average, GMI, how steady your days are, a real chart, your mornings vs. your nights, day by day, every low. Plus a page that ranks your own meals by what they did to you afterward. And a "why did this happen" page that shows its evidence.

REVIEW — this week against last week. Wins on one side, things worth raising on the other. Printable, so you walk into your appointment with a page instead of a shrug.

COPILOT — tell it what's going on in plain words. A guided symptom check. Your lab results explained in English. Appointment prep. A morning check-in.

PLAN — a week of meals, a recipe library, a grocery list built from the plan you chose, and your best meals pulled from your own readings.

MOVE — exercise ideas filtered by the time and energy you actually have today. Ten minutes and tired is an option.

TOOLKIT — questions for your doctor, appointment briefs, your labs, a travel packing checklist, everything the app has learned about you (readable, correctable, deletable), and a share link for whoever helps you.

SETTINGS — your targets, your units, export everything, delete everything.

What it is not: a medical device. Not a dosing calculator. It records insulin and medication and never once suggests a dose. Your numbers come from the app's own math, not from a chatbot guessing.

The nine-day drip

One screen a day

One idea per post, no link fatigue. If reach drops, put [LINK] in the first comment instead of the post.

01Today

Open the app and the first thing you see isn't a form. It's your last reading, where today sits, and one line about what needs attention. Logging comes second. [LINK]

02The photo

Nobody weighs their rice. So: take a picture of the plate, get a carb estimate, correct it if it's off. An estimate you'll actually make beats a perfect number you skip. [LINK]

03Trends

Time in range. Your average. Your mornings against your nights. Every low, listed. The chart your doctor asks for, without the shoebox of paper. [LINK]

04Meals ranked

This one surprised me. The app ranks your meals by what your glucose did after them. Not a food database's opinion. Yours. Sometimes the "healthy" one loses. [LINK]

05Review

Every week: this week against last. Wins on one side, things worth raising with your care team on the other. Hit print, bring the page. [LINK]

06Copilot

"I feel shaky and I don't know why." Type that. It reads your actual numbers back to you and walks you through a symptom check. It will never tell you to change a dose — that's your doctor's call, and the app is built so it can't. [LINK]

07Plan & Move

A week of meals, a recipe library, and a grocery list built from the plan you picked. Plus exercise ideas filtered by the time and energy you have today. "Ten minutes, exhausted" is a valid answer. [LINK]

08Share

Diabetes is rarely one person's job. One link shares your numbers with whoever helps — a partner, a daughter, a nurse. You pick what they see. [LINK]

09Your data

There's a page listing everything the app has learned about you. You can read it, correct it, or delete it. Export the whole thing whenever you want. That page exists because I'd want it. [LINK]

The replies you'll need

Comments, answered

These five questions arrive every time. Answer them in the thread, not in a DM — the thread is where the next person reads them.

Is it free?

Free while I'm building it out with real users. [PRICE]/month later, and I'll say so long before anything changes.

Does it replace my doctor?

No, and it's built not to. It doesn't diagnose and it never suggests a dose — it organizes your numbers so your appointment goes better.

Type 1 or type 2?

Both. You set your own targets, and it logs insulin and medication either way.

Where's my data?

Your own database, yours alone. Export it or delete it from Settings whenever you want.

I already use a CGM.

Then skip the typing — import the file from your sensor or meter and the trends fill in.
