# Steady — Facebook post pack

Everything the app does, written the way you'd say it to a person, in formats you can paste
straight into Facebook. Fill in the brackets: `[LINK]`, `[PRICE]`, `[CITY]`.

Two rules that apply to every post in this file, taken from what the app actually is:

1. **Never write a health outcome.** No "lowers your A1c", no "controls your blood sugar", no
   "know exactly what to eat". Steady shows you your own numbers and what they line up with. That
   is the promise. It is also the one you can keep.
2. **Never write anything about doses.** The app records insulin and medication and never suggests
   or changes a dose. Say that out loud — it's a trust feature, not a limitation.

And one timing note: the 43 items in the clinical knowledge layer haven't been reviewed by a
clinician yet. So post about Steady as a **tracking and pattern app** and hold the words
"clinical", "guidance", "advice" and "copilot that answers medical questions" until that review is
done. Same for taking money — an unreviewed knowledge layer behind a paid subscription is the
exposure. Organic posts describing tracking are fine today.

## 1. The one big post — "everything it does"

Paste as-is. Long posts work on Facebook when they're scannable.

> I built a diabetes app for people who are tired of logging numbers into nothing.
>
> It's called **Steady**. Your diabetes, one day at a time.
>
> Most apps are a diary. You type the number, the number goes in a list, and the list never tells
> you anything. Steady reads your own numbers back to you.
>
> Here's the whole thing:
>
> **TODAY** — your last reading, where today sits, the last 7 days, what needs attention, and the
> patterns your data actually supports. Not generic tips. Your Tuesdays.
>
> **LOG** — glucose, meals, insulin, movement, sleep, water, symptoms, weight, blood pressure,
> medication taken. Snap a photo of your plate and it estimates the carbs. Already wear a CGM?
> Import the file from your meter or sensor instead of typing.
>
> **TRENDS** — time in range, your average, GMI, how steady your days are, a real chart, your
> mornings vs. your nights, day by day, every low. Plus a page that ranks *your own meals* by what
> they did to you afterward. And a "why did this happen" page that shows its evidence.
>
> **REVIEW** — this week against last week. Wins on one side, things worth raising on the other.
> Printable, so you walk into your appointment with a page instead of a shrug.
>
> **COPILOT** — tell it what's going on in plain words. A guided symptom check. Your lab results
> explained in English. Appointment prep. A morning check-in.
>
> **PLAN** — a week of meals, a recipe library, a grocery list built from the plan you chose, and
> your best meals pulled from your own readings.
>
> **MOVE** — exercise ideas filtered by the time and energy you actually have today. Ten minutes
> and tired is an option.
>
> **TOOLKIT** — questions for your doctor, appointment briefs, your labs, a travel packing
> checklist, everything the app has learned about you (readable, correctable, deletable), and a
> share link for whoever helps you.
>
> **SETTINGS** — your targets, your units, export everything, delete everything.
>
> What it is not: a medical device. Not a dosing calculator. It records insulin and medication and
> never once suggests a dose. Your numbers come from the app's own math, not from a chatbot
> guessing.
>

## 3. The drip — one post per screen, nine days

Short, one idea each, no link fatigue. Post the link in the first comment if reach drops.

**Day 1 — Today**
> Open the app and the first thing you see isn't a form. It's your last reading, where today sits,
> and one line about what needs attention. Logging comes second. [LINK]

**Day 2 — The photo**
> Nobody weighs their rice. So: take a picture of the plate, get a carb estimate, correct it if
> it's off. An estimate you'll actually make beats a perfect number you skip. [LINK]

**Day 3 — Trends**
> Time in range. Your average. Your mornings against your nights. Every low, listed. The chart
> your doctor asks for, without the shoebox of paper. [LINK]

**Day 4 — Meals ranked**
> This one surprised me. The app ranks *your* meals by what your glucose did after them. Not a
> food database's opinion. Yours. Sometimes the "healthy" one loses. [LINK]

**Day 5 — Review**
> Every week: this week against last. Wins on one side, things worth raising with your care team on
> the other. Hit print, bring the page. [LINK]

**Day 6 — Copilot**
> "I feel shaky and I don't know why." Type that. It reads your actual numbers back to you and
> walks you through a symptom check. It will never tell you to change a dose — that's your
> doctor's call, and the app is built so it can't. [LINK]

**Day 7 — Plan and Move**
> A week of meals, a recipe library, and a grocery list built from the plan you picked. Plus
> exercise ideas filtered by the time and energy you have *today*. "Ten minutes, exhausted" is a
> valid answer. [LINK]

**Day 8 — Share**
> Diabetes is rarely one person's job. One link shares your numbers with whoever helps — a partner,
> a daughter, a nurse. You pick what they see. [LINK]

**Day 9 — Your data**
> There's a page listing everything the app has learned about you. You can read it, correct it, or
> delete it. Export the whole thing whenever you want. That page exists because I'd want it. [LINK]

## 5. The comment replies you'll need

**"Is it free?"**
> Free while I'm building it out with real users. [PRICE]/month later, and I'll say so long before
> anything changes.

**"Does it replace my doctor?"**
> No, and it's built not to. It doesn't diagnose and it never suggests a dose — it organizes your

**"Type 1 or type 2?"**
> Both. You set your own targets, and it logs insulin and medication either way.

**"Where's my data?"**
> Your own database, yours alone. Export it or delete it from Settings whenever you want.

**"I already use a CGM."**
> Then skip the typing — import the file from your sensor or meter and the trends fill in.
