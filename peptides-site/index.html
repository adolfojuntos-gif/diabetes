How does it work?

Your body makes a hormone called GLP-1 after you eat. It tells your brain you are full. These medicines copy that hormone and help it last all week.

Helps you feel full

Signals your brain that you have had enough, so smaller meals feel satisfying.

Keeps you full longer

Slows how fast food leaves your stomach, so you snack less between meals.

Steadies blood sugar

Helps your body release insulin when you eat, with fewer highs and crashes.

Could it be right for you?

Weekly GLP-1 treatment is usually for adults who have:

  • A BMI of 30 or higher, or
  • A BMI of 27 or higher plus a weight-related health problem, like high blood pressure, high cholesterol, or prediabetes

A licensed provider makes the final decision after looking at your health history.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the same as Ozempic® or Wegovy®?

Ozempic and Wegovy are brand names for semaglutide. Mounjaro and Zepbound are brand names for tirzepatide. Your provider prescribes the medicine that is right for you, and you will see exactly which product you are getting before you pay.

How fast does it work?

Many people feel less hungry in the first week or two. Weight comes off slowly as the dose goes up. Most weight loss happens between months 4 and 12.

What are the most common side effects?

Nausea, diarrhea, and constipation. They happen most when the dose goes up and usually get better. Your care team can help.

Does the shot hurt?

Most people feel only a small pinch. The needle is very short and thin.

Important Safety Information

Warning: possible thyroid tumors, including cancer

In studies with rodents, semaglutide and tirzepatide caused thyroid tumors, including cancer. It is not known if they cause these tumors in people. Do not use them if you or any of your family have ever had medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), or if you have Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2). Tell your provider if you get a lump or swelling in your neck, trouble swallowing, a hoarse voice, or shortness of breath.

Possible serious side effects

  • Pancreatitis. Stop and call your provider if you have severe stomach pain that does not go away, with or without vomiting.
  • Gallbladder problems, such as gallstones.
  • Low blood sugar, especially if you also take insulin or a sulfonylurea.
  • Kidney problems. Diarrhea and vomiting can cause dehydration. Drink plenty of fluids.
  • Serious allergic reactions. Get help right away if your face, lips, or throat swell, or you have trouble breathing.
  • Changes in vision in people with type 2 diabetes, and faster heart rate.
  • Depression or thoughts of suicide. Call your provider if your mood changes.

Most common side effects

Nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, constipation, stomach pain, headache, tiredness, upset stomach, dizziness, bloating, burping, gas, and heartburn.

Before you start

Tell your provider about all your health conditions and medicines, and if you are pregnant, planning to get pregnant, or breastfeeding. Tell them if you are having surgery that needs anesthesia. Do not use these medicines with other GLP-1 medicines.

This is not all the information about these medicines. Talk with your provider and read the Medication Guide that comes with your prescription. You are encouraged to report side effects to the FDA at www.fda.gov/medwatch or 1-800-FDA-1088.
