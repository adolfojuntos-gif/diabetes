I

How it works

A natural "I'm full" signal, made to last longer.

When you eat, your gut releases a hormone called GLP-1. It tells your brain you have had enough. Semaglutide and tirzepatide copy that hormone and keep the signal going for a whole week.

Your brain

Gets the message that you are full, so smaller meals feel like enough.

Your stomach

Empties more slowly, so you stay full for longer after you eat.

Your blood sugar

Stays steadier after meals, with fewer highs and crashes.

VI

Taking your weekly shot

Under a minute, once a week. Always follow your prescription's directions.

  1. Wash your hands. Check the pen's label and date. The liquid should be clear. Attach a new needle.
  2. Choose a spot. Your belly (2 inches from your belly button), the front of your thigh, or the back of your upper arm.
  3. Clean the skin with an alcohol pad and let it dry.
  4. Set your dose. Turn the dial to the dose your provider prescribed. Never change it yourself.
  5. Give the shot. Press the needle straight in. Hold the button and slowly count to 6.
  6. Finish up. Needle into a sharps container. Pen back in the fridge.

VIII

Questions

Plain answers to what people ask most.

Is this the same as Ozempic® or Wegovy®?

Ozempic and Wegovy are brand names for semaglutide. Mounjaro and Zepbound are brand names for tirzepatide. Your provider prescribes the medicine that is right for you, and you will see exactly which product you are getting before you pay.

Who can get weight-loss medicine?

Usually adults with a BMI of 30 or more, or 27 or more with a health problem linked to weight, like high blood pressure or prediabetes. A licensed provider makes the final decision.

How fast does it work?

Many people feel less hungry in the first week or two. Weight comes off slowly as the dose goes up. Most of it happens between months 4 and 12.

What are the side effects?

The most common are nausea, diarrhea, and constipation. They happen most when the dose goes up and usually get better. Rare but serious problems include pancreatitis and gallbladder problems. Your provider will go over these with you.

Does the shot hurt?

Most people feel only a small pinch. The needle is very short and thin.

Do skin and collagen products need a prescription?

No. The serums are cosmetics and the collagen is a supplement. They ship right away.

Do you sell BPC-157 or TB-500?

No. They are not approved for people. The FDA limits making them, and they are banned in drug-tested sports. Our athlete products are legal, tested, and safe for competition.

IX

Important Safety Information

Please read this before you start.

Warning: possible thyroid tumors, including cancer

In studies with rodents, semaglutide and tirzepatide caused thyroid tumors, including cancer. It is not known if they cause these tumors in people. Do not use them if you or any of your family have ever had medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), or if you have Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2). Tell your provider if you get a lump or swelling in your neck, trouble swallowing, a hoarse voice, or shortness of breath.

Possible serious side effects

  • Pancreatitis. Stop and call your provider if you have severe stomach pain that does not go away, with or without vomiting.
  • Gallbladder problems, such as gallstones.
  • Low blood sugar, especially if you also take insulin or a sulfonylurea.
  • Kidney problems. Diarrhea and vomiting can cause dehydration. Drink plenty of fluids.
  • Serious allergic reactions. Get help right away if your face, lips, or throat swell, or you have trouble breathing.
  • Changes in vision in people with type 2 diabetes, and faster heart rate.
  • Depression or thoughts of suicide. Call your provider if your mood changes.

Most common side effects

Nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, constipation, stomach pain, headache, tiredness, upset stomach, dizziness, bloating, burping, gas, and heartburn.

Before you start

Tell your provider about all your health conditions and medicines, and if you are pregnant, planning to get pregnant, or breastfeeding. Tell them if you are having surgery that needs anesthesia. Do not use these medicines together with other GLP-1 medicines.

This is not all the information about these medicines. Talk with your provider and read the Medication Guide that comes with your prescription. You are encouraged to report side effects to the FDA at www.fda.gov/medwatch or 1-800-FDA-1088.
